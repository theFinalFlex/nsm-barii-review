# NSM Barii: evidence-based review of published work

**Cleaned research edition · evidence collected through September 6, 2026. Archive coverage remains incomplete.**

The strongest supported picture is of a developer progressing from Python networking exercises to wireless monitoring, IoT research and portable hardware integrations. The record shows published code, credited dependencies and collaborators, and documented revisions and failures. It supports practical development experience; broad reliability claims remain unvalidated. The clearest formal research recognition established here is the finder credit for **CVE-2025-15474**. ## How the work fits together

The earliest material shows a consistent practical interest: **collect network observations, store them, make them readable, and react to changes**. The first-week video describes a scheduled Nmap wrapper with local results and notification plans. NetSpeed applies the same pattern to speed measurements and historical comparisons. The latter measures favorable conditions; its “speed up” title does not establish increased bandwidth. [First-week account, 0:39–2:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SPyDKpf3YkQ&t=39s), [NetSpeed, 3:29–4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2eHa2hSzy6M&t=209s).

Two main software families develop from there. **NetVuln → NetVuln-2.0** grows from a port scanner into aggregation of external service information and AI interpretation. **NetAlert → NetAlert-2.0 → NetAlert-3.0/Yoda** grows from local device discovery and whitelist alerts toward a GUI, voice experiments and eventually broader radio monitoring. NetSniffer is a related local-visibility experiment; the 150-day video explicitly says it pivoted from an earlier Camera Scanner idea. These are documented connections, whereas treating every similarly themed repository as a formal successor would be speculation. [NetVuln-2.0 README](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/NetVuln-2.0), [Yoda archived README](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Yoda/blob/main/_archive/README.md), [NetSniffer pivot, 6:37–6:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wudeiKIpcOI&t=397s).

During late 2025 and 2026, the center of attention moves toward **Wi-Fi/BLE, consumer devices and physical builds**. NetCracker and framework explore wireless and IoT testing. Bluehound focuses on Bluetooth observations and interference heuristics. Current Yoda brings Wi-Fi and Bluetooth monitoring together; its source explicitly acknowledges Bluehound lineage and uses a tshark wrapper for Wi-Fi observations. **Vader and Maul** are a separate, explicitly paired infrastructure-inventory line, with public history of outside fixes. [Bluehound](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Bluehound), [Yoda monitor source](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Yoda/blob/main/src/nsm_monitor.py), [Vader](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Vader), [Maul](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Maul).

The **Flock-related projects serve three distinct roles**:

| Project | Practical role | Main evidentiary limit |

| **Dooku**, formerly linked as **war-rig** | Packages a Raspberry Pi, multiple radios, GPS, power and custom dashboard/scripts around capture tools including Kismet. | A documented integrated build; field accuracy, endurance and completeness are not benchmarked here. |

## What technical depth is supported

The evidence supports practical experience combining Python libraries, network protocols, external APIs, persistent results, interfaces, concurrency and hardware. The dated record moves from simple scripts toward modular components and multiple cooperating processes. Selective current Yoda inspection confirms CLI arguments applied before startup and notification destinations disabled by default; these support specific implementation claims, not overall reliability. His videos also document the act of debugging rather than presenting only polished outcomes. [Yoda entry point](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Yoda/blob/main/src/main.py), [default variables](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Yoda/blob/main/src/nsm_vars.py), [port-scanner corrections, 10:00–10:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gD5j6zYpKzg&t=600s).

His most distinctive recent work in the reviewed material is **integration across software and physical equipment**: separate radios, GPS, capture services, a dashboard and a car display form a coherent workflow. That credit should coexist with clear attribution to Kismet, the hardware vendors and community research. Writing the integration and interface is meaningful work; it does not imply invention of the underlying radio protocols or capture engines. [Dooku documentation](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/Dooku).

A stronger claim—production-grade security engineering, accurate autonomous vulnerability discovery, or reliable jammer classification—would require evidence not yet established here. For example, a count of devices that disappear can be useful anomaly evidence, but it does not itself isolate jamming from ordinary movement, interference, power management or capture limitations. The longer Bluehound video explicitly says its “radar” does not provide direction. The edited Yoda video shows acknowledged latency and inconsistent elapsed-time announcements. [Bluehound explanation, 2:54–3:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HOf1A88ozNo&t=174s), [Yoda demonstration, 3:23–4:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7OyHeHakheE&t=203s).

The videos document several limitations at specific stages of development:

- **NetBruter:** in the 100-day video, he says the apparent successes he reviewed were false positives. [8:59–10:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KmQQo4xG-LU&t=539s).
- **NetVuln:** the dedicated video acknowledges unreliable NVD responses and external API limitations. [6:12–8:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DNEvb71kFA4&t=372s).
- **NetVuln-2.0:** the 200-day video says vulnerability checks and supplying directory output to AI are not yet integrated, despite broader project ambitions. [12:30–13:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ph6newsu3xo&t=750s).
- **NetCracker:** a staged voice claim is explicitly retracted in that same video. Later commits and the January 2026 Short describe subsequent additions; version timing matters. [3:35–4:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ph6newsu3xo&t=215s), [January clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OSmh4S1Vv0o).
- **NetAlert-2.0:** its final July 2025 commit explicitly ends development and addresses background-thread shutdown. This is stronger evidence of retirement than simply an old last-push date. [Commit](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/NetAlert-2.0/commit/1ffa8fd).

Some educational statements need further checking. The automatic RF-channels transcript says **39 BLE channels**, whereas Bluetooth SIG specifies **40**. Audio verification is still needed to distinguish a transcription error from a spoken error. The passive-observation Short recommends hiding the SSID, but Apple explains that hidden names do not conceal network detection and can introduce privacy exposure. Broad household-vulnerability percentages in the Wi-Fi videos lack a cited sampling method. These discrepancies should be assessed individually and checked against the original audio. [RF Short](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=olHiAJvIDHk), [Bluetooth SIG](https://www.bluetooth.com/learn-about-bluetooth/tech-overview/), [passive-observation Short](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uNs9aeZixB4), [Apple guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102766), [Wi-Fi video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PpAucZ52NSw).

## Research credit, collaborators and sponsorships

**CVE-2025-15474 is verified formal credit.** The record names VulnCheck as the assigning authority and credits Jabari Lucien (nsm_barii). It describes resource exhaustion affecting availability in an AuntyFey lock and assigns CVSS v4.0 **5.3 / Medium**. The record was published **January 7, 2026**, although the identifier uses 2025. The authority advisory is dated January 6. The repository’s broad smart-lock context must not turn this into a generalized claim of unlocking arbitrary locks or proving the separate bypass repository. [Official record](https://github.com/CVEProject/cvelistV5/blob/main/cves/2025/15xxx/CVE-2025-15474.json), [VulnCheck advisory](https://www.vulncheck.com/advisories/auntyfey-smart-combination-lock-ble-connection-flood-dos).

The separate smart-plug, smart-lock-bypass and router repositories remain sparsely documented research artifacts in this review. The Adorcam write-up explicitly requires authentication for its exposed Telnet service; it is not evidence of an authentication bypass or another assigned CVE. Failure to find corroboration in this pass is not proof that none exists. [Camera write-up](https://github.com/NSM-Barii/ip-camera-exposed-telnet), [research inventory](https://github.com/NSM-Barii).

Outside contributions are substantive enough to preserve individually. **Chocapikk’s NetCracker PR #1 and Vader PR #1 were merged on March 19, 2026**. Maul merged Barii’s follow-up PR #2, which credits preceding Chocapikk work; the earlier PR itself was not merged. Later Maul/Vader entry-point refactors remain open. A closed flock-back contribution was not merged. This distinction prevents both erasing contributors and crediting every proposed change as adopted. The specific Barii contribution still needs a comparison with upstream. **BLE Shark is a third-party sponsored product**: the July 26 description acknowledges sponsorship and a supplied device, while his repository contains portal customizations rather than the original product firmware.
